📋 OVERVIEW — August 28, 2026

Late-August action around Marco Island is settled into classic summer patterns — mangrove snapper are the reliable workhorse on nearshore structure and dock pilings, while redfish and snook tuck into mangrove shade lines and creek mouths. Tarpon are still around as the summer run winds down, and charters are running early to beat near-daily afternoon storms back to the dock.

Inshore

▼
Redfish✓ GOOD
Holding tight to mangrove shade on higher tide stages. Cut ladyfish or cut mullet on the shoreline, or a weedless gold spoon worked slow along the roots.
Snook🔥 HOT
Prime around creek mouths, points, and dock lights at night. Live pilchards and pinfish on the outgoing tide. Season is closed — release everything.
Speckled Trout✓ GOOD
Stacked on grass flats in 2–4 feet on the incoming. Popping cork with live shrimp is the go-to; DOA shrimp works when the wind lays down.

Nearshore (≤5mi)

▼
Mangrove Snapper🔥 HOT
The most reliable player right now. Anchor on hard bottom or artificial reefs inside five miles, knocker rig with a live shrimp or small pilchard. Bring a cooler.
Spanish Mackerel✓ GOOD
Working chum slicks and bait pods on the nearshore reefs. Small silver spoons and long-shank hooks with light wire — they cut through mono.
Tripletail✓ GOOD
Sight-fishing floating debris and channel markers on the ride in and out. A live shrimp pitched past and drifted back rarely gets refused.

Backwater

▼
Tarpon✓ GOOD
Late-summer stragglers in the Ten Thousand Islands and around passes at first light. Fly rods or live crabs. Catch-and-release year-round.
Juvenile Tarpon🔥 HOT
Backcountry creeks and residential canals holding rolling fish 15–40 lbs. DOA Baitbuster or a small streamer on the shadow line — vicious eats.
Sharks✓ GOOD
Blacktips and bonnetheads cruising the deeper backcountry holes. Cut ladyfish on a wire leader, chunked on the bottom during moving water.
