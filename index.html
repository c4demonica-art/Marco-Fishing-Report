📋 OVERVIEW — September 4, 2026

The early fall pattern is showing up right on cue around Marco Island, with redfish pushing off the mangroves onto open flats and points while snook stack at creek mouths ambushing bait on the outgoing tide. Warm, green-blue Gulf water is holding baitfish tight to the beaches and nearshore wrecks, keeping mangrove snapper, mackerel, pompano and the tail end of the summer tarpon run all in play.

Inshore

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Stacked at creek mouths and mangrove points on the outgoing tide. Live pilchards and topwater plugs walked at first light are drawing serious eats.
Redfish🔥 HOT
Sliding off tight cover onto open flats, points, and island edges. Cut bait, gold spoons, or a live shrimp under a popping cork all producing.
Speckled Trout✓ GOOD
Filling grass edges in 3-5 ft during moving water. DOA shrimp and popping cork rigs are the workhorse setup.

Nearshore ≤5mi

▼
Mangrove Snapper🔥 HOT
Loaded on wrecks and reefs inside 5 miles. Cut sardines and small pinfish on light fluorocarbon leader are the difference-maker.
Spanish Mackerel✓ GOOD
Blowing up glass minnows along the beaches. Silver spoons, chuggers, or small jigs on a fast retrieve trigger reliable strikes.
Pompano✓ GOOD
Sandy troughs and beach edges are producing. Sand fleas and yellow banana jigs bounced along the bottom.

Backwater

▼
Tarpon✓ GOOD
Tail end of the summer run along the outside beaches at daybreak. DOA Baitbuster or a live threadfin still moving fish.
Black Drum✓ GOOD
Holding around dock pilings and oyster bars. Live shrimp on a light jighead does the job.
Sheepshead· SLOW
Starting to trickle in around structure but numbers still thin. Fiddler crabs and live shrimp for the picky biters.
