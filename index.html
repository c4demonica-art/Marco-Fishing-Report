📋 OVERVIEW — May 22, 2026

Tarpon migration is in full swing along the Marco and Naples beaches, with silver kings rolling in the passes at first light and big snook pushing toward the outside points ahead of the spawn. Backcountry redfish are tailing on the low water, tripletail are starting to show on the crab pot floats, and sharks are sharing the same water as the tarpon for nonstop bent-rod action.

Inshore

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Big pre-spawn fish staging on outside points, passes, and beach troughs. Live pilchards and large flair-hawk jigs at dawn and dusk are doing the damage.
Redfish🔥 HOT
Upper-slot to over-slot reds chewing on oyster bars and grass edges. Cut ladyfish on the bottom and weedless gold spoons up shallow on the higher tides.
Speckled Trout✓ GOOD
Solid numbers over deeper grass flats in 3 to 6 feet. Popping cork with a live shrimp or paddletail on a quarter-ounce jighead is the move.

Nearshore (≤5mi)

▼
Tarpon🔥 HOT
Peak migration window — pods rolling along the beach in calm conditions. Live threadfins or pass crabs drifted ahead of moving schools is producing jumps every trip.
Sharks🔥 HOT
Blacktips and bulls shadowing the tarpon schools and chewing cut bait on the bottom. Heavy tackle a must — these fish are pulling drag hard.
Tripletail✓ GOOD
Starting to show on crab pot floats and channel markers. Idle along, spot the fish, then pitch a live shrimp under a float just past it.

Backwater

▼
Backcountry Snook✓ GOOD
Smaller resident fish still chewing in the creeks and along the mangrove edges. Twitchbaits and small white jigs on the falling tide are dependable.
Mangrove Snapper✓ GOOD
Steady action around docks, oyster points, and creek mouths. Live shrimp on a small jighead with light fluoro is hard to beat.
Jack Crevalle· SLOW
Bigger schools have thinned out as water warms. Scattered fish busting bait in the creeks at first light — topwater plugs when you find them.
