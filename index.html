📋 OVERVIEW — May 29, 2026

Tarpon season is in full swing across Marco Island and Naples as the silver kings continue their migration along the beaches and through the passes, with consistent rolling pods at first light in calm conditions. Snook are staging on outside points ahead of spawn, redfish are scattered on warming flats, and trout, tripletail, and sharks round out a fired-up inshore and nearshore bite.

Inshore

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Big females staging on outside points and pass edges pre-spawn. Sight-fish on a clean incoming tide with live pilchards or a slow-worked DOA Shrimp.
Redfish✓ GOOD
Scattered on warming flats. Look for tailing fish on higher water around oyster bars and mangrove edges — cut mullet or a weedless gold spoon will get bit.
Sea Trout✓ GOOD
Steady on the grass flats in three to five feet. Live shrimp under a popping cork is hard to beat; jerkbaits in white or pearl will cover water on slower mornings.

Nearshore

▼
Tarpon🔥 HOT
Migration in full swing along the beaches and through the passes. Live threadfins, pass crabs, and big shrimp on 40-65lb braid with heavy fluoro leader and circle hooks at first light.
Sharks🔥 HOT
Blacktips and bulls running with the tarpon schools. Cut bonita or ladyfish on heavy tackle, fished off the beach in 10-20 feet, will get crushed.
Spanish Mackerel✓ GOOD
Feeding on bait pods just off the beach and around the markers. Silver spoons, Gotcha plugs, and small white jigs on a fast retrieve will fill a cooler.

Backwater

▼
Snook✓ GOOD
Holding on mangrove points and creek mouths well back in the system. Live shrimp or a small pilchard on an outgoing tide gets the bite — work the shade lines.
Juvenile Tarpon✓ GOOD
Rolling in the backcountry bays and creek systems. Small swimbaits, DOA Baitbusters, and live finger mullet on 30lb leader will draw eats from the 10-40 pound class.
Tripletail· SLOW
Hit or miss around channel markers and crab pot floats. Free-lined live shrimp pitched cleanly is the play — keep your eyes peeled on the run.
