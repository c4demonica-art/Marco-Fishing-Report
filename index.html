📋 OVERVIEW — September 11, 2026

September is living up to its reputation as Marco Island's best fishing month — redfish are schooling on the flats, snook are stacked around the beaches and passes, and juvenile tarpon in the 40-pound class are keeping fly rods bent from Rookery Bay to the Ten Thousand Islands. Warm, glassy mornings and light east winds have made bait plentiful and sight-fishing conditions about as good as they get.

Inshore

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Stacked along the beaches and passes at first light. Live pilchards or white paddletails on a 1/8 oz jighead — work the wash and shadow lines.
Redfish🔥 HOT
Overslot fish schooling on outside oyster bars in the Ten Thousand Islands on falling tides. Cut ladyfish, gold spoons, and weedless soft plastics.
Speckled Trout✓ GOOD
Solid numbers on deeper grass edges in 4–6 ft. Popping cork and shrimp is the easy button; jerkbaits at first and last light for the bigger ones.

Nearshore (≤5 mi)

▼
Mangrove Snapper🔥 HOT
Hard-bottom spots 2–5 miles out are loaded. Chunked pilchards or shrimp on a light knocker rig with 20 lb fluoro — pick your window between afternoon storms.
Goliath Grouper✓ GOOD
Wrecks and artificial reefs holding fish in the 100–200 lb range. Live blue runners or ladyfish, heavy tackle, quick fights and release.
Kingfish· SLOW
Scattered smokers on the deeper edges but not stacked yet. Trolled spoons or live threadfins if you find them; cool fronts should push them thicker in a few weeks.

Backwater

▼
Juvenile Tarpon🔥 HOT
40 lb class fish rolling in the creeks and bays behind Marco and Rookery Bay. Small dark flies at dawn; DOA Baitbusters or live pinfish under a cork the rest of the day.
Sheepshead & Black Drum✓ GOOD
Turning on early this year around dock pilings, seawalls, and Caxambas Pass rocks. Live shrimp on a knocker rig, fluoro leader, sharp small hook.
Tripletail✓ GOOD
Hanging on crab trap buoys and channel markers. Idle slow, spot them, pitch a live shrimp on a jighead — they usually eat on the first drift.
