📋 OVERVIEW — October 30, 2026

End-of-October water has cooled another notch across Marco Island, Goodland, and the Ten Thousand Islands, and the fall pattern is in full swing — snook pinned to pass drop-offs, redfish schooling tight on the flats, and the first real wave of sheepshead stacking on dock pilings. A midweek cold front dropped lows into the sixties and lit up the trout bite, while scattered juvenile tarpon are still rolling at dawn in the backcountry creeks.

Inshore

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Pass drop-offs and dock lights going strong. Live pilchards and finger mullet daytime, white flare hawks after dark. Big fish holding deep on the outgoing.
Redfish🔥 HOT
Big schools stacked on negative lows against the mangrove points. Weedless gold spoons, cut mullet, and live shrimp. First light is prime time.
Spotted Seatrout🔥 HOT
Cold front has them absolutely chewing. Shrimp under popping corks on the knee-deep grass of Johnson, Rookery, and Hurricane Pass. Gator trout mixed in at dawn.
Pompano✓ GOOD
Showing on the sandbar edges and in the passes. Yellow or pink pompano jigs, small sand fleas on a dropper. Fast-growing fishery through the fall.

Nearshore (≤5mi)

▼
Spanish Mackerel🔥 HOT
Blitzing bait pods on 3-5mi numbers. Fast-trolled spoons, gotcha plugs, and small flashy jigs. Watch the frigate birds.
Gag Grouper✓ GOOD
Season still open through November on the Gulf. Live pinfish and large flat lines over the wrecks and hard bottom in 20-40ft.
Mangrove Snapper✓ GOOD
Holding tight on the artificial reefs. Small knocker rigs with cut sardines or live shrimp. Fluorocarbon leader and go light.
Kingfish✓ GOOD
Scattered keepers on the 5-mile hard bottom. Slow-trolled threadfins and blue runners with a stinger rig. Window closing as the water keeps cooling.

Backwater

▼
Sheepshead🔥 HOT
First real push onto dock pilings and bridge supports. Live shrimp or fiddler crabs on a 1/8-ounce jig. Numbers climbing fast with every cool front.
Snook🔥 HOT
Deep creek bends and residential dock lights after dark. White flare hawks, DOA shrimp, live pilchards along the mangroves.
Redfish✓ GOOD
Schooled on the flats and tight to the mangroves on high water. Weedless gold spoons, cut mullet, and chunked ladyfish on the oyster bars.
Juvenile Tarpon· SLOW
A few still rolling in the back of Caxambas and Ten Thousand Islands creeks at dawn, but numbers thinning fast. Baitbusters and small threadfin flies.
