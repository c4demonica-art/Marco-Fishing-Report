📋 OVERVIEW — November 13, 2026

The fall turnaround is in full swing around Marco Island and Naples, with cooler mornings pushing redfish onto the flats and snook holding tight to mangrove edges at first light. Thanksgiving-week trips are stacking up fast as juvenile tarpon sightings taper and the first real sheepshead push arrives on nearshore structure and dock pilings.

INSHORE

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Mangrove edges and creek mouths at first light. Topwater plugs early, then live shrimp on 20 lb fluoro once the sun climbs. Slot fish are thick.
Redfish🔥 HOT
Oyster bars and open flats on moving water. Cut mullet, gold weedless spoons, and soft plastics all drawing strikes — look for tailing fish on the late incoming.
Sea Trout✓ GOOD
Deeper grass flats in 4–6 feet. Jig-and-shrimp combos and DOA shrimp under a popping cork filling coolers steadily.

NEARSHORE (≤5mi)

▼
Mangrove Snapper✓ GOOD
Nearshore rock piles and artificial reefs inside five miles. Small pinfish and live shrimp on light fluoro — chum them up and the bite ignites.
Spanish Mackerel✓ GOOD
Working bait pods just off the beaches. Silver spoons and gotcha plugs on fast retrieves — follow the birds for easy coolers.
Sheepshead· SLOW
First push starting to show on channel markers and pilings as water temps drop. Fiddler crabs beating shrimp — bite should pop within two weeks.

BACKWATER

▼
Jack Crevalle🔥 HOT
Blowing up mullet pods in the creeks and bays. Topwater walkers and gold spoons both drawing explosive strikes all day long.
Snook🔥 HOT
Mangrove shorelines and creek mouths at dawn. Fish are aggressive and moving as fronts push water temps down into the mid-sixties.
Juvenile Tarpon· SLOW
Sightings tapering as cold fronts roll through — still rolling in deeper backcountry channels on warmer afternoons. Get them while you can.
