📋 OVERVIEW — July 24, 2026

Peak summer pattern in full swing across Marco and Naples — snook are stacked on the passes, redfish are locked to the mangrove shade, and juvenile tarpon are rolling deep in the backcountry creeks. Water is clear tropical green with light morning wind, so plan on early launches, plenty of water, and topwater at first light before the heat shuts the bite down.

Inshore

▼
Snook🔥 HOT
Spawning aggregations on Big Marco Pass and Caxambas — flair-hawks and live pilchards after dark, topwater at first light on mangrove points.
Redfish✓ GOOD
Tight to the mangrove roots on high water — weedless gold spoons and shrimp-tipped jigs. Best action on the last two hours of the incoming tide.
Speckled Trout✓ GOOD
Deeper grass edges 4-6 feet — popping corks with live shrimp keep filling the box. Larger gator trout on top-water at dawn.

Nearshore ≤5mi

▼
Tarpon🔥 HOT
Migration schools cruising the beach in 8-15 feet — live crabs on the tide changes, threadfins from a chum slick. Best window: sunrise to 10 a.m.
Mangrove Snapper✓ GOOD
Stacked on nearshore ledges and wrecks — cut threadfin on light fluoro leader. Keepers to 16 inches steady.
Kingfish· SLOW
Scattered singles on the outer edge — water's a touch warm inshore. Try slow-trolled ribbons early before the sea breeze fills.

Backwater

▼
Juvenile Tarpon🔥 HOT
Rolling in deep creek bends and dead-end bays through the Ten Thousand Islands — DOA Baitbusters and small crabs get eaten. 20-50 pound class.
Snook (backcountry)✓ GOOD
Small-to-mid slot fish tucked deep in the mangroves — flip weedless jerkbaits into the shade pockets on the falling tide.
Goliath Grouper✓ GOOD
Big boys parked under backcountry bridges and creek holes — heavy tackle, live ladyfish, quick photo and release.
